# Event-Linked Coconut Farm Rehabilitation Planning

COCO-AID v2.4 links rehabilitation planning to forecast or simulated extreme-weather periods. Each event creates a separate management heatmap and schedule.

## Farmer decision sequence

1. Wait until authorities and field conditions permit safe access.
2. Conduct a mapped inspection of crowns, trunks, roots, drainage, standing water, pests, and tree stability.
3. Separate safety and sanitation work from agronomic rehabilitation.
4. Restore drainage after extreme rain, or conserve moisture after drought and heat.
5. Identify palms likely to recover before replacing them.
6. Use quality planting material and local extension guidance for replanting.
7. Reinspect at 30 and 90 days.

## Map classes

- **No Damage:** continue routine monitoring.
- **Needs inspection:** field verification is required before action.
- **Needs Rehabilitation:** prioritize a documented work order after field verification.

## Timing

The default field-inspection date is three days after an event ends. The default rehabilitation start is seven days after the event ends. These dates are planning defaults and must be adjusted for safety, access, soil saturation, and local agriculture-office advice.

## Evidence basis

The workflow is informed by the Philippine Agricultural Training Institute's Coconut Specialist Course, including use of farm layouts, field inspection, corrective-action timelines, and good agricultural practices. It also follows FAO coconut-sector guidance emphasizing systematic replanting, suitable planting material, diversification, and sustained extension support.

## Limitation

The heatmap is derived from event severity, projected loss, farm condition, slope, drainage, tree-state vulnerability, and a smooth event footprint. It is not a satellite-observed damage map. Red and yellow zones require field confirmation.
